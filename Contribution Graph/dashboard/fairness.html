Fairness Rules and Boundaries

How contributions, commissions, and disputes work, including current limitations.

Back to Project Dashboard

How contributions enter the ledger

Members submit contributions and evidence for independent review by another project member. Only verified contribution value can be minted. Each contribution’s evidence can be recorded once, and each task’s total minting cannot exceed its administrator-set cap. Reviewers cannot verify their own contributions. During a dispute, related scores and identifiable Token payments are paused until a qualified administrator who is not a party to the dispute decides the outcome.

How commissions settle

A commission reserves a task’s minting budget, but the reserve itself does not charge the principal. After delivery and independent approval, the system mints the verified value and pays the agreed contract price. A final dispute outcome can release payment to the contractor, refund the principal in full, or split the payment by explicit amounts. Refunds and splits only process the original frozen payment; they do not mint again or restore used task capacity. Cancelling an unsettled contract only releases its reserve and does not create a payment refund.

Benefits and costs

Contributors can earn Tokens for verifiable work. Principals can retain the difference between task value and payment price, while contractors receive the negotiated payment. Independent review and dispute handling add waiting time and require members to spend time reviewing and explaining disputes. Administrators must maintain task caps, evidence, and the ledger.

Current boundaries

  • A two-person project cannot independently approve a commission; add a third reviewer.
  • If a contractor transfers payment away, the ledger may not be able to freeze the full amount. The system rejects settlement and shows the outstanding amount instead of debiting other members.
  • Freezes only cover identifiable related payments in the ledger and cannot recover off-ledger assets.
  • Contribution units can change with task caps, review decisions, and practice. Token amounts do not represent market price, hours worked, or fair wages.
